Every angle is a projected angle — accurate only when the plane of the gesture faces the camera. See Protocol.

Capture protocol

  • Camera perpendicular to the plane of the gesture being measured — a tilted plane biases every angle by up to several degrees and shortens every length.
  • Put the calibration reference in that same plane, at the subject's distance from the camera, not closer or farther.
  • Keep a reasonable minimum distance from the subject — close-up shots exaggerate perspective error.
  • Avoid the wide-angle lens/zoom level — wide angle increases perspective distortion at the frame edges.

What this measures, and what it doesn't

angle-lab reads a projected angle and a scaled length off a single 2D photo. It does not reconstruct 3D geometry. Following the protocol above keeps the projection error small; it cannot remove it.

  • Angles are immune to distance/scale, but not to the gesture's plane being tilted relative to the camera: roughly up to 8° of error at a 30° tilt.
  • Lengths need a correct scale (mm/px), which only holds in the plane of the calibration reference — a limb 30 cm closer to or farther from the camera than that plane reads 20–38% off. A tilted (foreshortened) limb reads short: roughly 13% short at a 30° tilt.
  • The on-screen quality colour (green/amber/red) reflects placement precision only — how exactly the points were tapped, at what zoom. It does not know about, and cannot warn about, camera tilt or distance.
  • angle-lab measures and reports. It does not diagnose or recommend.
